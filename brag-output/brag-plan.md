# brag-plan: Reprange, vertical

## Angle

Reprange is a workout tracker that deliberately does one thing. The angle is
restraint: a phone that logs a set in two taps, starts its own rest timer, and
shows a progress line. Not a "complete fitness platform". The joke that earns
its place is the footer line the app already uses about itself.

## Who it is for

Somebody who wants to log what they lifted and get on with their session,
without configuring a dashboard first.

## What sets it apart

Setup is four questions and nothing to import. The rest timer starts the moment
a set is logged. Progress is a real one-rep-max line against every session
since you started.

## Hook (0:00 to 0:03)

Near-black, the mark resolving into "Reprange", then the one line a stranger
needs: **Tracks what you lift. Nothing else.** The Today screen fades up behind
it, held back at half strength so the product is visible from the first frame
without competing with the words. Everything after this is proof, not a claim.

## The beats, in the product's own order

Entry -> key action -> result, which is literally how the app works:

1. **Open** — the Today screen, one thing to do.
2. **Log a set** — the stepper row, weight and reps, and the "Log set" button.
   This is the "two taps" claim, shown rather than stated.
3. **Rest starts itself** — the timer strip appears with the set name on it.
   The claim from the landing page, demonstrated by the behaviour it names.
4. **The result** — the progress line: estimated one rep max per lift, drawn
   against every session.
5. **Punchline** — the footer, verbatim: "Reprange · tracks what you lift,
   nothing else". Then the wordmark.

## Tone

`default`. Punchy, clean, four-plus beats, soft fades. The product is quiet
and confident, so the video should not shout over it. No fake startup tropes,
no invented testimonials, no numbers that are not in the app.

## Visual identity, taken from the source

Dark theme, because the product is used in a gym and on a phone at night.

- `--bg: #0c0c0d` background
- `--surface: #161618` panels, `--surface-2: #1e1e21` raised
- `--ink: #f5f5f5` primary type, `--ink-2: #a8a8ae` secondary
- `--line: #262629` hairlines, `--fill: #232326` chips
- `--success: #69db7c` the one accent, used only for the progress line and the
  logged state
- Quicksand, the app's real typeface, at 500/600/700

Ink on ink at these values is the app's own contrast, so nothing here is a new
colour invented for the video.

## Storyboard, 20.0s total, 1080x1920, 30fps

| # | Scene | Dur | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 3.0s | Wordmark resolves, then "Tracks what you lift. Nothing else." The Today screen fades up behind it at half strength. |
| 2 | Open | 3.0s | Today screen: the one thing to do. The card rises, the two logged rows stagger in after it. |
| 3 | Log a set | 4.0s | Weight and reps steppers, the "Log set" button presses, the row lands. This is the two-tap claim. |
| 4 | Rest starts itself | 4.0s | The rest strip arrives carrying the set name, the countdown runs down. |
| 5 | The result | 3.6s | The estimated 1RM line draws across sessions in --success, then the readout lands. |
| 6 | Punchline | 2.4s | "Reprange" then the footer's own words, "tracks what you lift, nothing else". |

Sum: 3.0 + 3.0 + 4.0 + 4.0 + 3.6 + 2.4 = 20.0s

Every scene boundary is a dip through the background, six frames out and six
frames in, so two busy layouts never sit on top of each other. Each scene also
eases up 46px as it settles, so a held frame still has somewhere to go.

## The one number in the video

The Progress readout says **Best est 1RM 99.2 kg**, which is what
`estimate1RM` in `src/lib/coach.ts` returns for the 85 kg x 5 set the video just
logged (`85 * (1 + 5/30)`), rounded the way `toDisplay` rounds it. It is the
app's own arithmetic, not a number chosen to look good.

## Readability budget

Checked rather than assumed. `work/check.py` samples every scene and asserts
each run of copy is settled with at least 0.3s per word left before the scene
ends, after the dip. The tightest line in the cut is "wondering how long is
left." at 1.8s for five words.

## Sound

One piece, not a bed with effects on top. A low pulse on A holds the whole 20
seconds, breathing at 0.5Hz and sitting back as the video moves into the
product beats. Three soft ticks in the same key land on beats the viewer can
see: the set being logged at 7.2s, the rest strip arriving at 10.06s, the
progress line finishing at 16.16s. They climb A4, E5, A5 and never rise above
the pulse.

## How it was built

The frames are SVG generated by `work/frames.py`, one pure function of a scene
progress value, rasterised with cairosvg and encoded with ffmpeg. Quicksand is
the app's own face: `work/woff2ttf.py` converts the project's `.woff` files
into the TTFs the renderer can actually resolve, so the video is set in the
same typeface as the app rather than in whatever fallback was on the machine.

- `work/check.py` layout, collisions and readability, from measured boxes
- `work/pixels.py` the stills read back as pixels, including whether the
  renderer really used Quicksand or silently fell back
- `work/verify.py` the finished `brag.mp4` pulled back out and measured
